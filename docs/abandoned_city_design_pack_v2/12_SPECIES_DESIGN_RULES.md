# Species Design Rules

## Resolved Creative Rule

Nature should feel **alien but biologically plausible**.

The goal is not random mutation. Every strange feature should appear to solve a problem.

## Scientific Freedom

Choose:

- strict realism
- plausible speculative evolution
- science-fiction freedom with biological logic
- mostly fantasy

Recommended:
**science-fiction freedom with biological logic**

Keep / change: keep

## Mutation Causes

Current premise:
- pre-Fallout genetic engineering existed
- extreme radiation affected surviving ecosystems
- 2,000 years of adaptation followed

Should anything else contribute?

Answer: nope that should be enough to make it interesting to explore

## Recognizable Ancestry

Should players sometimes recognize modern ancestors?

Recommended: Yes

Example:
A creature is strange, but careful observation suggests it descended from a deer, crow, rat, dog, fish, insect, etc.

Keep / change: keep

## Mutation Range

Current target:
3–4 / 5

Meaning:
- clearly evolved descendants
- sometimes highly alien-looking
- rarely completely unrecognizable

Keep / change: keep

## Design Rule: Adaptation First

Every species should answer:

1. What did it evolve from?
2. Where does it live?
3. What environmental pressure shaped it?
4. What does its mutation help it do?
5. How does it interact with another species?

Keep / change: keep

## Plant Rules

Define 5 rules.

1. Every plant has a recognizable ancestor in its silhouette (leaf shape, flower, bark), even when scale or color is strange.
2. Plants use the city as a resource: metal, concrete, glass, water in basements. Their growth explains how the ruins look.
3. At least one visible reaction per plant (closing, turning, glowing, sound) that the player can trigger or wait for.
4. Size follows light and water: the tallest growth is where sun reaches and water collects; dark interiors get small, pale or glowing species.
5. Plants are part of a relationship (pollinator, food, shelter, host), so their pages cross-link to at least one other entry.

## Animal Rules

1. Ancestry is readable on a second look: body plan and movement come from the ancestor; mutations change size, armor, senses or coloring.
2. Every animal has a daily rhythm (where it is by day vs. night) so the time of day matters for observing it.
3. Animals react to the robot as an unknown object: most are cautious, a few are curious, dangerous ones warn before they act.
4. Danger always telegraphs (sound, posture, territory markers) and can always be escaped by leaving, climbing or hiding.
5. Every animal has one behavior worth waiting for, which is the condition that completes its page.

## Fungi Rules

1. Fungi are the main decomposers of the city and are found where wood, paper and bodies of old animals used to be.
2. Spores are the main danger tool: visible, avoidable clouds with a clear warning.
3. Parasitic fungi change host behavior in observable ways (odd movement, climbing high to release spores), which makes infected animals their own Logbook discovery.

## Color

How colorful can organisms become?

Answer: some accent colors can be more colorful and playful

## Bioluminescence

How common?

Answer: about 10% of species should have developed bioluminescence 

## Scale

Smallest organism visibly catalogued: insects at around 1cm

Largest plant: 100m tall, mutated wisteria and seqoia

Largest animal: 20m tall

## Intelligence

Can some species display advanced intelligence?

Yes

Describe: ants form systematic colonies where there are larger pray around so they can eat the leftovers. fungi developed further and can infest animals and make them become their spore dispenser.

## Symbiosis

How common should symbiotic relationships be?

Answer: common

## Dangerous Species

Since the player cannot fight, dangerous organisms should create traversal / observation problems rather than combat encounters.

Possible danger types:
- territorial
- venomous
- ambush predator
- spore cloud
- swarm
- parasitic
- environmental manipulation
- nesting defense

Answer:
- territorial
- venomous
- ambush predator
- spore cloud
- swarm
- parasitic
- environmental manipulation
- nesting defense

## Signature Species

The first five signature species are the MVP species. Full details are in `17_MVP_SPECIFICATION.md`.

### Species 1
Name / placeholder: Lantern Wisteria
Ancestor: wisteria
Type: plant (climbing, bioluminescent)
Habitat: hanging from overpasses and balconies, Old Town
Mutation: glowing seed pods
Behavior: pods glow at night
Ecological role: food and shelter for Lantern Moths, which pollinate it

### Species 2
Name / placeholder: Hookbill
Ancestor: crow
Type: bird
Habitat: torii gates and rooftops
Mutation: goose size, hooked beak, grasping feet
Behavior: collects shiny human objects for its nest
Ecological role: scavenger; spreads seeds of fruiting plants

### Species 3
Name / placeholder: Drainback
Ancestor: rat
Type: mammal
Habitat: storm drains and flooded basements
Mutation: armored back plates
Behavior: hides at movement, forages when everything is still
Ecological role: prey for larger predators; eats fallen seeds and insects

### Species 4
Name / placeholder: Vault Mantis
Ancestor: praying mantis
Type: insect, ambush predator
Habitat: dark ground-floor interiors
Mutation: dog-sized, camouflage matching mossy tiles
Behavior: guards an egg case, warns with a wing rattle before striking
Ecological role: top predator of the block; hunts Drainbacks

### Species 5
Name / placeholder: Lantern Moth
Ancestor: moth
Type: insect
Habitat: underside of overpasses by day, Lantern Wisteria by night
Mutation: hand-sized, glowing markings
Behavior: night swarms around glowing pods
Ecological role: pollinator of Lantern Wisteria; food for Hookbills at dawn
